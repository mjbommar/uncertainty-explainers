How evidence becomes a forecast | Uncertainty Explainers 2

--- DESCRIPTION (3531/5000) ---
A forty-year-old woman gets a routine breast cancer screening, and the result is positive. In a 1982 study, 95 of 100 doctors put her chance of cancer between 70 and 80 percent. The correct answer was about 8 percent. Where does a probability come from, and how can we test one?

A probability sums up evidence. It is not a hidden fact waiting inside the world. Five stops show how to build one honestly: name the group being counted (the reference class, with Laplace's sunrise), update from the base rate with Bayes' rule (one thousand women, one test), use a range when evidence is thin (Ellsberg's urn), say what kind of claim about the future you are making (Hume, Lorenz, and a 50-run weather ensemble), and score many forecasts to check them (the Brier score and calibration).
